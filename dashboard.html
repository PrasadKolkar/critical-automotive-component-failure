01 / Understanding the data

Most readings show ordinary operation.

Issue examples are rare. The three component labels are combined into one task: identify whether a reading has an issue.

Only 1.78% of readings carry an issue label.

03 / Follow-up experiments

Did changing the training setup help?

Further experiments examined sensor selection, PCA, the ABS indicator and additional training examples.

ANN: choosing the inputs

Feature selection kept useful sensors. PCA combined inputs into fewer components.

Selected sensors reduced false alerts from four to one. PCA produced six false alerts and missed four issues.

XGBoost: adding training examples

Sampling was applied inside training groups. Held-out readings stayed unchanged.

Sampling bars show mean F1 across seeds 42, 7 and 2026. The ranges show the lowest and highest seed results. Sampling runs use unweighted XGBoost; the comparison includes both original controls.

Neither sampling method exceeded the recorded weighted XGBoost result.

What about the ABS indicator?

Models were also evaluated with the ABS fault indicator. High scores remained when it was removed, so this single indicator does not explain the leading results.

05 / What this means

Strong classification results. An open real-world question.

Several models recognised the supplied issue labels well. But the data are synthetic and contain only 35 issue readings. Sensor cutoffs chosen after seeing all labels also separated the component cases perfectly.

This study does not establish advance warning of real breakdowns. That next step needs real vehicle histories, confirmed failures and a defined warning period.
